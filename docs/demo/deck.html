How a check works

Sealed work, a deadline, a core count.

01A fresh problemSeeded INT8 matrices, 8.8 trillion operations a step, on the tensor cores. Nothing to precompute.
02A clock on our sideThe deadline runs on the API, sized to the listed GPU's rated speed.
03Sealed, then re-gradedThe GPU commits every answer first. Then we pick 8 rows and recompute them on a CPU.
04Count the coresA timing staircase: 132 on an H100 SXM, 108 on an A100. Heat can't remove cores.
PASSThe listed chip, on time. Published at once.
DEGRADEDThe listed chip, too slow. Published with its numbers.
FAILAnother chip, or wrong answers. Published with the listing.

Every failure carries its listing

A pass needs real silicon. A failure needs the listing you rented.

The listing
  • The reporter pastes the listing they rented
  • Read automatically: a different GPU stops the report
  • Shown beside the failure for anyone to compare
Two reports to fail
  • One report: suspect. Two: failed, on the GPU and its provider
  • Suspicious reports are flagged automatically
  • Two passes after a failure: recovered
Each report is its own vote03
